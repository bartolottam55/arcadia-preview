Welcome home

This is Arcadia.

Heaven, down to earth.

In Greek myth, Arcadia is a peaceful utopia, a paradise. Your home may be a small hill in the vast prairie of life, but we’re honored to be in the picture: a part of the puzzle, the shepherds of shelter while you keep gathering the pieces.

We hope you enjoy your time here, for however long, and that you find your own Arcadia somewhere inside.

Now, enough with the peaceful pasture references. This is Chicago. And we (yourself included) are not sheep.

A building built after 1929?

Never heard of her.

You’re living in an artifact of one of the world’s great architectural cities. When you pay rent, you’re contributing to a true piece of history. We’re weirdos about it, and when you live here you’re invited to be as weird as you want about anything. Encouraged, in fact. These buildings are alive with stories, and we’ve made it our mission to preserve them. Go ahead and put your ear to the bricks. Don’t worry about who’s watching.

This kind of character comes with quirks.

We call them amenities.

Floors are strong. Not always straight.

Stand a penny on its edge and see if it rolls. No? Congrats, you’ve got a lucky level one. If it rolls, congrats: you’ve got a lucky penny.

Squeaks and creaks. Not ghosts.

This is the soundtrack to the soul of the building. Sing along. Your home isn’t haunted; it’s just the real, original wood talkin’.

Neighbors are neighborly. Not always nice.

If we could, we’d take personal responsibility for the impeccable vibe of every tenant. But we have boundaries. We recommend you do, too.

Insects and rodents suck. Unfortunately, they also exist.

We’ve forged an experienced army against unwanted critters. But if you’re running for the hills at first sight, consider running for the suburbs.

New stuff breaks more than old stuff did.

If it were built in 1929, it wouldn’t break. We’d complain all day about how they don’t make ’em like they used to, but we’re busy fixing appliances.

We actually trust the MEPs of today.

Two things we didn’t want to keep from 100 years ago: the pipes and the wiring. Our mechanical, electrical and plumbing systems can handle your sh*t.

Not your father’s landlording.

The idea of being lord of anything gives us the ick. We are, for all intents and purposes, a bunch of regular guys. Regular guys who buy old buildings, obsess over every detail of the redesign, and occasionally ask our girlfriends to write manifestos that include phrases like “for all intents and purposes.”

Arcadia has one founder who can remember George W. but not George H.W. He has three partners who joined over the first few years, helping build the plane while it flew wildly around Chicago’s neighborhoods. If we were smarter, we’d have hired a property management company. But then you’d be dealing with a deadbeat property manager instead of reading this.

Leadership is here, involved, and likely the ones knocking on your door asking to copy your key. Our construction team reminds us that the American dream lives on through people who show up grateful and ready to get the job done. Our building engineers have often been caring for the property since before you were stacking Legos. To them, residents are family. Robert has a habit of showing up every morning to prune the bushes and pick up trash. We’re not even sure we’re paying him for it.

We operate with full transparency and love to show how the sausage is made. Not to make it weird, but we’re very proud of our sausage. The work is never easy or pretty, but we don’t believe in cutting cornices. We believe in preserving them to their highest integrity, in honor of the craftsmen who came from all over the world, found home, and built our city. That requires dirty hands. All on deck.

The team at Arcadia takes personal responsibility for the heart, soul and bones of our buildings, so their stories can live on through yours.

Whether you’re looking for a slice of heaven or occasionally raising hell, Arcadia welcomes you home.

Where you'd be living

Neighborhoods we actually know.

We only buy on blocks we would live on ourselves — walkable, near a train, close to good food and a park.

Old Irving Park

Independence Park, a summer farmers market, and some of the best-kept Victorians in the city. The Blue Line at Irving Park gets you downtown in twenty minutes.

Humboldt Park

Two hundred acres of park with a lagoon in the middle, wide boulevards built for strolling, and the greystones that line them.

Logan Square

The boulevard, the Blue Line, and a farmers market that takes over the square every Sunday. Walkable in every direction.

Avondale

Along the river just north of Logan, quieter and a little easier on the wallet. Milwaukee Avenue runs the length of it.

West Town

Between the parks and the river, with Chicago Avenue through the middle. Landmark blocks of brick and greystone.

Lakeview

Tree-lined side streets a few blocks from the lake, with the Brown Line close by.

Jefferson Park

The Blue Line terminus and a Metra stop, so the commute is the easy part. Bungalow blocks and big trees.

Uptown

The lakefront on one side, Argyle Street on the other, and more live music per block than anywhere in the city.
