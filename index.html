Where you'd be living

Neighborhoods we actually know.

We only buy on blocks we would live on ourselves — walkable, near a train, close to good food and a park.

Old Irving Park

Independence Park, a summer farmers market, and some of the best-kept Victorians in the city. The Blue Line at Irving Park gets you downtown in twenty minutes.

Humboldt Park

Two hundred acres of park with a lagoon in the middle, wide boulevards built for strolling, and the greystones that line them.

Logan Square

The boulevard, the Blue Line, and a farmers market that takes over the square every Sunday. Walkable in every direction.

Avondale

Along the river just north of Logan, quieter and a little easier on the wallet. Milwaukee Avenue runs the length of it.

West Town

Between the parks and the river, with Chicago Avenue through the middle. Landmark blocks of brick and greystone.

Lakeview

Tree-lined side streets a few blocks from the lake, with the Brown Line close by.

Jefferson Park

The Blue Line terminus and a Metra stop, so the commute is the easy part. Bungalow blocks and big trees.

Uptown

The lakefront on one side, Argyle Street on the other, and more live music per block than anywhere in the city.
